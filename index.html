No exceptions

Metal, I take. Always.

Rusted, bent, broken, buried in the weeds — doesn't matter. Appliances, pipe, fencing, roofing tin, car parts, water heaters, batteries, a pile of nothing-in-particular. Metal never goes in a hole in the ground if I can help it. Call me on that one anytime.

What I pick up

Good things get a second life

Cleaning out a garage, a rental, a shop, an estate? Most of what people call junk isn't. A dresser, a working fridge, a stack of lumber, a toolbox — somebody out there still needs it. I take it and I make sure it gets used, rehomed or recycled.

Yes — I'll come get it

Still has life in it

If you'd feel a little bad setting it on the curb, that's usually the right instinct.

  • Any metal at all — no condition requirement
  • Furniture in usable shape — dressers, tables, chairs, beds, cabinets
  • Appliances that run, or are worth fixing
  • Light fixtures, fans, doors, windows, hardware
  • Leftover building materials — lumber, block, trim, fencing
  • Tools and yard equipment
  • Whole-room or whole-house clear-outs of the above
Not what I'm after

I'm not a dumpster

I'd rather tell you now than show up and turn you down.

  • Household garbage and bagged trash
  • Rotted, soaked or mold-damaged furniture
  • Demo debris, torn-out drywall, shingles, rubble
  • Paint, chemicals, fuel, tires, hazardous material

Not sure which side yours lands on? Send the photo anyway — I'll give you a straight answer.

The deal

Usually free. Always up front.

The whole point of this is keeping good material in use and out of a landfill, so free is where I start. Here's the honest math on when it isn't.

Free — the default

Good things cost you nothing

Usable, fixable or recyclable — I want it and you don't pay a dime. That's the deal I'd rather make every time.

When there's a charge

A little over what the dump costs

Some loads carry real junk, and the landfill charges me by weight plus fuel to get there. If that's your load, I'll quote you before I come out — a bit over what the dump run and gas actually cost me. Nothing hidden, nothing added later.

Good offsets bad

Value in the load changes the math

If there's something worth having mixed in, I'll often eat the disposal cost and haul the whole thing anyway. Tell me everything that's going — the good stuff is what makes the rest work.

Barter welcome

I'll trade

Cash isn't the only way to settle up. I'll trade for items and I'll trade for work — hauling against something you need done, or something you can do that I need. Fair on both ends, let's make a deal.

Licensed contractor or GC? I work as your sub. You hold the license, I bring the hands and the experience.

Under a homeowner permit

You can pull the permit on your own property and I'll do the work. I'll tell you exactly what to pull and what the inspector will be looking for.

It passes inspection

Done to code. If an inspector red-tags something I put in, I come back and fix it — you don't pay twice for that.

And your vehicle

Light automotive — maintenance, brakes, suspension, diagnostics, tune-ups. Basic to fairly advanced. I'll tell you straight whether it's something I should touch or something that belongs at a shop.

Three things I don't take on: roofing, plumbing and transmissions. The first two aren't my trade. The third I've done plenty of, and I'm done doing it. Anything else — I've either done it or I'll learn it.
